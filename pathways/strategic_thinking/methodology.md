# Strategic Thinking — Methodology

## Purpose

This Pathway develops strategic thinking in Change Management practitioners. It is not a substitute for change models, frameworks, or formal change-management training. It is a developmental lens that helps practitioners move from execution toward insight.

## Core Concepts

### Strategic vs. Tactical Thinking

- **Tactical thinking** focuses on what to do, by when, and with whom.
- **Strategic thinking** focuses on why the change matters, what it is connected to, what the choices imply, and how to influence direction.

A strong strategic thinker is still able to execute. This Pathway does not devalue execution; it builds the thinking that informs execution.

### Systems Thinking

A change touches multiple parts of an organization at once. Systems thinking means looking at how people, processes, technology, and structure interact rather than treating the change as a single task stream.

### Dependencies

Dependencies are the relationships that determine whether a change can proceed as planned. They include people who must be aligned, processes that must be ready, technology that must be available, and decisions that must come first.

### Second-Order Effects

A first-order effect is the immediate, intended result of a change. A second-order effect is the indirect or delayed consequence, such as a shift in workload, morale, reporting relationships, or customer experience.

### Stakeholder Perspectives

Different groups see the same change differently. Strategic thinking requires understanding those perspectives without collapsing them into a single view.

### Tradeoff Analysis

No change is costless or riskless. Tradeoff analysis makes the costs, risks, and benefits of competing options explicit so decisions can be made transparently.

### Business Outcome Framing

A strategic recommendation should connect to business outcomes the organization values: revenue, risk reduction, customer satisfaction, operational efficiency, compliance, talent retention, or similar.

### Scenario Thinking

Scenario thinking means asking "what if" and exploring how the change could play out under different conditions. It prepares the practitioner for uncertainty.

### Organizational Implications

Every change creates ripples. Organizational implications include culture, power, communication, capacity, and readiness.

### Executive Decision Framing

Sponsors and executives need concise, relevant, and actionable guidance. Strategic framing answers: what is the decision, why does it matter, what are the options, what are the tradeoffs, and what is recommended.

## Pathway Knowledge vs. Domain Knowledge

This `methodology.md` contains pathway-specific strategic-thinking concepts. General Organizational Change Management knowledge — such as ADKAR, Kotter, Prosci, or stakeholder-engagement methods — belongs in the `Organizational Change Management` Information Domain and is not reproduced here.
